# Strategic BPMN Rationale

This strategic BPMN model was created to provide a high-level overview of how the Bristol Regional Food Network operates and creates value for its stakeholders. Rather than focusing on detailed operational workflows, the model highlights the organisation’s key business processes, stakeholder interactions, and organisational responsibilities. This approach is appropriate for the early stages of enterprise analysis, where the aim is to understand how the organisation functions as a whole and identify the capabilities that are most important to its success.

The model is divided into four main functional areas:

- **Producer and Product Management**
- **Sales and Operations**
- **Finance**
- **Quality, Compliance and Sustainability**

Using separate lanes helps to show how responsibilities are distributed across the organisation while also demonstrating how different functions work together to achieve shared objectives. This provides a clear view of the organisation’s value chain and the relationships between its core activities.

A key feature of the model is the use of **collapsed subprocesses**. Activities such as growing the producer network, managing the product catalogue, fulfilling orders, and ensuring food quality are represented at a high level rather than being expanded into detailed workflows. This was a deliberate modelling decision to maintain a strategic focus. At this stage of the project, the objective is to identify the organisation’s major business capabilities rather than document every task, decision, or exception within a process. Using collapsed subprocesses keeps the model clear and easy to understand while indicating that these activities can be explored in greater detail during later stages of analysis and system design.

The main process begins with demand for local food, which drives activities such as expanding the producer network and maintaining the product catalogue. These activities are strategically important because the organisation relies on strong relationships with local producers and a diverse range of products to meet customer demand. The process then moves into sales and order fulfilment activities, which represent the organisation’s primary value-creating function by connecting local food producers with consumers.

Finance is shown as a separate function to highlight its importance in supporting the long-term sustainability of the network. The interaction with the payment provider demonstrates how customer payments are processed and how producers are compensated for their products. The successful delivery of food and payment of producers represents the primary outcome of the process.

The model also includes quality assurance, compliance, and sustainability activities. These were included because the success of the Bristol Regional Food Network depends on more than commercial performance alone. Food safety and certification activities help maintain compliance with regulations and build consumer trust, while sustainability reporting reflects the organisation’s commitment to environmental and social responsibility. Including these processes ensures that the model captures both operational and strategic priorities.

External stakeholders, including producers, customers, delivery partners, payment providers, certification bodies, and food safety regulators, are represented in separate pools. This reflects the fact that the organisation operates within a wider ecosystem and relies on collaboration with external organisations to deliver value.

One limitation of the model is that it presents activities in a largely linear flow and does not include detailed decision points or alternative pathways. However, this was an intentional choice to preserve clarity and maintain a strategic perspective. As a result, the model should be viewed as a representation of high-level organisational capabilities and stakeholder relationships rather than a detailed operational process model.

Overall, the BPMN provides a clear strategic overview of the Bristol Regional Food Network, identifying the key processes, stakeholders, and responsibilities that support its objectives. It also helps identify two major business capabilities for further investigation and development: **Producer and Product Management** and **Customer Order and Fulfilment Management**. These capabilities will form the foundation for subsequent requirements analysis, i* modelling, backlog development, and system design activities in later sprints.
